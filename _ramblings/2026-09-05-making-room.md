---
title: "Some shmuck's perspective on the SWE identity/job crises"
intro: "Some folks were doomed from the beginning"
---
From what I can see, many, many SWEs have had complete identity crisis' as AI has gotten better
and better, writing more and more of their code. This is especially true for those who
are just now coming into the workforce. I've seen a lot of more senior folks talk
about how, surviving in this new era requires one to be the driver of "taste" and
to understand the systems you are working on. While what they are saying is correct,
I think it misses on why this shift has had such an effect on a lot of people.

Of course I was not around at that point, but I'm sure when calculators first came
around, accountants and the "human calculators" has 2 different reactions. They 
either adapted to their new reality, or they resisted. In terms of who succeeded,
would you trust an accountant who refused to use a calculator for your math? Assured
you they would do it all in their head or on pen and paper? That should answer
that question.

You don't talk to a financial advisor or accountant because you can't do the math or
because you can't do analysis. You talk to them because they have the experience and
skillset to analyze the math, perform the analysis, take into account your own situation,
and give you a personalized plan/conclusion suited to your situation. You are paying
them to synthesize information with the context of their knowledge, experience, and
**intuition** to give you a good plan.

Given this context, thse SWEs who spent their career being "code monkeys" (not meant as derogatory), being those
who just implement the feature/spec that they are told to, a tool coming along that
can now write code that is good enough to implement the feature/spec that someone is
can easily throw those SWEs' job prospects and identity for a twist. I am not excluded
from this. I too felt I brought value in my ability to program, and as AI improved and 
write good enough code far faster than I, and especially in languages/frameworks that I 
have no expereince in, I felt that value leave. That's why I changed paths and pursued away
from pure SWE. Of course now the AI is a hacking machine and is coming for cybersecurity
too, but cybersecurity as more nuance then just hacking stuff, and having the ability to
hack is not the same as being able to generate real value, judgement, and lower risk through
that hacking.

At this point everyone knows that the prevelance and intelligence of LLMs as changed and
will continue to change how thr workforce and humanity operates. Some run, some embrace,
some pretend like it never even happened. It will be interesting to see how things 
continue to change and adapt, how the economy handles everything, etc. I won't lie and 
say that I am terribly optimistic as to how they next couple years will play out, but
I will do my best to adapt and take on whatever comes down the pipe. All that being said:

If we want to keep our jobs, we have to create value (blech) and leverage the things that we have
that LLMs don't. Physical form, and being an actual human.
